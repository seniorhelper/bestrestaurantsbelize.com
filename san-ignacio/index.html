Where to eat in San Ignacio and Cayo

Western Belize’s busy market town is the base for Maya ruins, caves and rivers, and it eats well.

San Ignacio sits on the Macal River in the Cayo District near the Guatemalan border. It is where travelers sleep before trips to Xunantunich, Caracol and the caves, and its streets have a lively mix of Belizean, Mestizo, Chinese and backpacker-friendly kitchens. The Saturday market is the place for produce and snacks.

Dinner

Guava Limb Cafe is the town’s best-known restaurant, with a garden setting and candlelit dinners. Just outside the center, Running W at the San Ignacio Resort Hotel is praised for refined cooking in a jungle setting and is open from breakfast to dinner.

Breakfast and local plates

Pop’s Restaurant is the classic breakfast stop, according to Frommer’s the most popular in the area, with fry jacks, beans and eggs. Cenaida’s Belizean Food is a frequent pick for stew chicken with rice and beans.

On the way north: Orange Walk

If you are traveling to Lamanai or the north, Nahil Mayab in Orange Walk Town is a comfortable, air-conditioned stop with a patio, serving Latin American dishes and Caribbean classics.

Tips

  • Tour days start early, so breakfast places open early.
  • Many small restaurants close on Sundays; check ahead.
  • For the Maya roots of Belizean cooking, read our sister site’s Maya food page.
