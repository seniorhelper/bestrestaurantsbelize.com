Where to eat in Hopkins

Hopkins is the heart of Belize’s Garifuna community, and the best place in the country to eat Garifuna food.

Hopkins is a seaside village on the southern coast, between Dangriga and Placencia, with a long beach and a strong Garifuna identity: drumming, language and food. Small restaurants line the main road and the beach, and resorts sit just south of the village.

Garifuna food

The dish to try is hudut: fish cooked in a coconut broth (sere) and served with mashed plantain. Innie’s Restaurant is a long-standing favorite for hudut and ereba (cassava bread), and reviewers mention drumming nights. Tugusina Garifuna on the main road is popular with locals. Hudut takes time to make, so local guides suggest ordering it ahead.

Learn more about the dishes on our sister site’s Garifuna food page.

Dinner by the sea

Chef Rob’s Gourmet Cafe builds a daily menu from fresh market products, including lobster, conch and ceviche, in a casual wood-walled room near the water.

Getting there

Hopkins is reached by a short side road off the Southern Highway, between Dangriga and the turn for Placencia. Many visitors combine a few nights here with Placencia to the south. Resorts south of the village run their own restaurants; the village itself is where to find Garifuna cooking.

Tips

  • Garifuna Settlement Day on November 19 is the biggest celebration of the year on this coast; expect festivities and plan meals early.
  • Hopkins is spread out; bicycles are the usual way to get around.
  • Many small places keep short hours and may close one day a week.

Questions

Should I order hudut in advance?

Local guides suggest it. Hudut is slow-cooked and some kitchens make it to order, so ask a day ahead or early in the day.
